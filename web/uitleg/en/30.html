The 30: at least 30% tree canopy in every neighbourhood

How much of a neighbourhood's land lies under a tree crown? Per neighbourhood, the area of all tree crowns was added up and divided by the neighbourhood's land area.

What is measured?

The paper asks for at least 30% canopy cover in every neighbourhood, and stresses that it is about the neighbourhood, not the city average. We calculate:

canopy cover = tree crown area ÷ land area × 100%

Steps

  1. Tree crowns. The crown polygons come from the NEO tree data (table BOMEN_KRONEN in the provincial database): per tree a polygon with the area of its crown (CROWN_AREA).
  2. Neighbourhoods and land area. The boundaries and the land area (oppervlakte_land_in_ha) come from the CBS Wijk- en Buurtkaart 2023.
  3. Add up the crowns per neighbourhood. Per district and per neighbourhood, the crown areas of all crowns that touch the area are added up (spatial query STIntersects).
  4. Divide by the land area. Crown area (m²) ÷ land area (m²) × 100%.
  5. Carry over to the map's areas. So that the 3, the 30 and the 300 are shown on the same municipalities, districts and neighbourhoods (2025), the crown and land area of each 2023 neighbourhood were distributed over the 2025 areas in proportion to the overlapping area. The percentage per area was then calculated again. For municipalities that is practically exact; for neighbourhoods that were split or merged since 2023 it is an approximation. An area only gets a value when at least half of it is covered by neighbourhoods with crown data; otherwise a sliver of a neighbouring municipality would speak for the whole area.

Choices and why

ChoiceWhyAlternative we did not choose
Per CBS neighbourhood The paper asks for 30% per neighbourhood. The CBS buurt is the smallest official unit that comes closest to that, and it is the same nationwide, so comparable between municipalities and provinces. A grid or a radius around every home: more precise per home, but no longer "the neighbourhood" of the rule.
Crown polygons, not tree points The rule is about cover, so about area. A tree point says nothing about the size of the crown. Number of trees per hectare.
Dividing by land area No trees grow on open water. A neighbourhood with a lake or river would otherwise come out unfairly low. Total area including water.
A whole crown counts if it touches the neighbourhood Simple and fast in the database. A crown on the boundary counts in both neighbourhoods. Clipping crowns at the neighbourhood boundary: more correct, and how the open-source version should do it (see limitations).
CBS 2023 The year the FME calculation was made with; the land area comes from the same map. —
Carrying over to the 2025 areas When switching between 3, 30 and 300 on the map you want to see exactly the same polygons, so you can compare them. Showing each indicator on its own edition (as the map first did).
Colours: < 10, 10–20, 20–30, ≥ 30% 30% is the rule's threshold; the steps in between show how far a neighbourhood is from it. —

Limitations and open issues

  • Double counting on boundaries. A crown hanging over a neighbourhood boundary counts in full in both neighbourhoods. In small neighbourhoods that weighs heavily: one neighbourhood comes out above 100% (282%).
  • Missing values due to an error in the calculation. In 115 neighbourhoods (2023) with land, the calculation found no crown at all, although there are trees (in Noordkethelpolder, Schiedam, for example over 2,700). The crown data is complete; the cause is in the FME calculation. It looks up an area's boundary in the CBS map of 2022, using the code from the map of 2023. Neighbourhoods and districts that got a new code in 2023 are then not found: exactly 114 of the 115 (the last one is the beach of Katwijk, really without trees). It concerns all of Voorne aan Zee (formed in 2023, 70 neighbourhoods), 28 neighbourhoods in Schiedam (new division in 2023), 7 in Westland, 5 in Leiderdorp, 3 in Pijnacker-Nootdorp and 1 in Delft. Where a boundary moved between 2022 and 2023 but the code stayed, the calculation counts the crowns within the old boundary and divides them by the new land area; that can give deviating values too. The open-source version of the 30 solves this. On the map an area only gets a value when at least half of it has crown data; otherwise it is grey ("no data"). That is why the municipalities of Schiedam, Leiderdorp and Voorne aan Zee are grey. For Westland and Pijnacker-Nootdorp the value was calculated on the part with crown data.
  • Made with FME and a non-public database. The calculation can therefore not yet be repeated by others. The plan is to do it with open software (see the overview).
  • The date of the crown polygons follows the NEO delivery; the reference date is not recorded in the calculation.
